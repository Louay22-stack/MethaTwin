# Phase 1: market and technical research notes (2026-11-20)

Source IDs ([S#], [J#], [L#]) refer to `sources.md`.

## 1. Landscape in 2026

### Biogas and biomethane (the largest pool of matching jobs for this profile)

- **EU.** The REPowerEU goal of 35 bcm of biomethane by 2030 is out of reach. IFRI projects about 21 bcm, and only 15 Member States have quantified targets [S6]. The IEA forecasts EU output at about 62 % of the goal by 2030 [S5]. Even so, the sector is growing fast. EU biomethane grew 19 %/yr in 2020–2025 [S5], and Europe's installed capacity passed 8 bcm/yr in 2026 (+17 %) [S4]. Europe had 1,620 biomethane plants at the end of 2024 [S3].
- **France** is the largest market for grid injection. At end 2025 it had 803 sites and 15.5 TWh/yr of capacity [S1]. PPE3 targets 44 TWh in 2030 [S2], which means roughly tripling capacity. However, additions fell by 40 % in 2025 [S1], so cost reduction and project optimisation are the main issues. A plant optimisation and TEA project fits this directly.
- **Germany.** The 2025 Biomassepaket pushes flexible operation of existing biogas plants [S10].
- **Spain.** The PNIEC target is 20 TWh of biogas in 2030, and a 6 % biomethane quota is proposed for 2035 [S11].
- **Canada.** Quebec requires 5 % RNG in distributed gas in 2025, rising to 10 % in 2030. RNG volumes registered under the Clean Fuel Regulations rose 417 % in 2024 [S12]. A 1.4× credit multiplier for domestic RNG has been proposed [S13].
- **Economics.** The average EU production cost is about €75–80/MWh. Small plants cost much more than large ones (about €87/MWh at ~540 Nm³/h against about €54/MWh above 1,200 Nm³/h), and the French median LCOE is about €130/MWh [S6]. Cost is therefore the central problem, and an LCOE model has a validation target in these figures.
- **Biogenic CO2** is becoming a co-product. 125 European plants captured 1.17 Mt in 2025, compared with 0.16 Mt in 2020 [S6]. Capture costs €25–90/t [S6].

### Industrial biotechnology and fermentation

- The **EU Bioeconomy Strategy** (27 Nov 2025) covers more than 17 million jobs [S7] and identifies biomanufacturing scale-up as a gap [S8]. The **EU Biotech Act** part 1 was proposed in Dec 2025, and a part 2 on industrial biotech is announced [S9].
- **Fermentation capacity** is a known bottleneck for precision fermentation [S15].
- **Digitalisation.** Hybrid (mechanistic + ML) models and digital twins are the leading theme, and the shortage of digitally skilled staff is cited as a barrier [L6].

## 2. Skills employers ask for

This is a synthesis of the J1–J10 postings, not a statistical count.

1. **Mass and energy balances, equipment sizing, process simulation.** HYSYS, Aspen Plus, ProSim or equivalent appear in almost every process role [J6, J7, J8].
2. **Biogas and méthanisation know-how.** Upgrading, purification, digester operation and feasibility studies [J6, J9, J10].
3. **Fermentation scale-up and bioreactor operation.** This is the core requirement for bioprocess roles [J1, J3].
4. **Data analysis in Python, DoE and statistics.** A differentiator in most ads [J1, J2], and required in data-engineering and digital-twin roles [J4, J5].
5. **Kinetic modelling, digital twins, ML and chemometrics** [J4, J5].
6. **Techno-economic thinking.** Cost, LCOE and feasibility work [J10, S6].

**Implication.** The most valuable portfolio piece for a junior profile combines items 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 in a single believable industrial case. Item 3 (lab scale-up experience) cannot be shown without a lab and is out of scope by design.

## 3. Literature gaps relevant to the chosen project

- Several 2026 papers compare ML with ADM1 for *prediction* [L4] or use ML surrogates of ADM1 for *control* [L5]. No peer-reviewed paper was found that couples an ADM1 digestion model, an upgrading flowsheet, biogenic-CO2 recovery and plant-level TEA through an ML surrogate for *design and operating optimisation*. This was a limited search, so the claim is "not found", not "does not exist".
- Open-source ADM1 code ring-tested against BSM2 exists [L2, L3], which gives a solid validation anchor.
- The upgrading literature gives reference values for energy use and methane slip, but the units differ between sources [L7, L8]. Validation must state the basis used (raw gas or product gas).

## 4. Separation from the unpublished manuscript

The user's unpublished ML-for-AD manuscript is **not** used, cited or described anywhere. This project uses only:

- **simulated** data generated by a public mechanistic model (ADM1), and
- published literature parameters.

Its ML part is a *surrogate of a mechanistic model* for optimisation. It does not predict empirical plant data.
