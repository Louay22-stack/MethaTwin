MethaTwin · design concept v4 (revision B) · for approval

Dark, quiet, precise.
One amber signal.

Revision B, after your feedback: MethaTwin becomes a dark, premium instrument. Warm graphite surfaces (not the old navy), no gradients and no glow, and one electric-amber signal reserved for the brand, the primary action and “where to look”. Calm comes from restraint: generous space, two fonts and very little colour. The brand text and the page map below are unchanged.

1 · Brand

New slogan
See your plant work
before it exists.

Alternatives: “Run it here first.” · “Every choice, tried first.”

Brand promise

A biomethane plant is decided once and runs for twenty years. MethaTwin gives anyone, farmer, investor or engineer, a faithful copy of that plant to try ideas on: change the feed, the tank or the price of power, and it tells you in plain words whether the microbes stay healthy, whether the gas is fit for the grid and whether the plant pays, with every answer traceable to tested science and public data.

Positioning

For people deciding on a farm biomethane plant, MethaTwin is the try-before-you-build twin that turns biology, gas cleaning and money into one clear answer, unlike spreadsheets that skip the biology or simulators that only experts can drive.

2 · Logo sketch: the twin monogram “M”

An M drawn as two mirrored strokes that meet at one node in the valley. The left stroke is solid and heavy (the real plant); the right stroke is its mirror, drawn as a dotted blueprint line (the twin). One plant, two versions, joined at a single point: the model. Single colour, legible at 16 px, and it works as an app icon on an amber tile.

3 · Design system

Graphite 950#121212 · canvas
Graphite 900#1B1A18 · cards
Graphite 850#25231F · raised, inputs
Signal amber#FFB224 · brand, primary action, focus · 10.4:1
Bone#F3EFE6 · text 16:1
Stone#A09A8E · helper text 6.7:1
Statusok #5DD39E · warn #FF8F5A · bad #FF6B7A, always with icon + word
Chart seriesamber · sky · sage · rose (all ≥7:1)

Type: Sora for headlines, verdicts and big numbers (geometric, confident) · Manrope for everything else, with tabular figures for values. No monospace and no serif: two fonts only. Base 16 px, nothing below 12 px.

Surfaces: flat graphite cards, 1 px hairlines, 16 px radius, depth from tone steps rather than shadows or blur. Amber appears at most once or twice per screen. Input borders use #6E685D (≥3:1) so controls stay visible.

Tokens: three layers. Primitives (graphite 950…100, amber 50…900, status hues, 4-pt space scale, radius 8/12/16/24, motion 120/200/320/600 ms) → semantic (--surface-canvas, --text-muted, --status-ok-fg, --action-primary-bg, --focus-ring) → component (--verdict-bg, --reveal-border, --nav-item-active…). One Python module generates the CSS and the chart template from them.

Motion: fade/rise 200 ms on reveal, 320 ms page entry, the home animation loops gently; everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion. Focus: 3 px amber ring with 2 px offset on every control.

4 · Home page: “The living core” (3D)

A new three.js scene, not a whole-plant model this time: a single digester core shown as a cutaway. A vertical cut plane divides it: on one side shaded graphite metal with feed particles drifting down; on the other, its twin as an amber wireframe where the particles become rising gas bubbles. Drag to orbit (horizontal only, so phones can still scroll the page), or drag the cut plane to show more real or more twin. Three hotspots (Biology, Gas, Money) light up parts of the core and show one plain sentence each, matching the three answers on “Your plant”. Technical care: instanced particles, pixel ratio capped at 2, rendering paused off-screen or in hidden tabs, keyboard control of the cut plane and hotspots, a static poster if WebGL is unavailable, and a still frame under reduced motion. The scene is purely illustrative and shows no model values.
